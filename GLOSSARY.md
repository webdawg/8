# Glossary — Dark Matter Manifolds

Terms defined over the course of building `dark-matter-manifolds.html`, sourced from the original prompts in `llm-original-prompt/`. This is the settled definition of the three things on screen.

## Left Manifold
A calculus manifold — a flat plane shape that crosses multiple dimensions, represented in 3D (x, y, z), continuously rotating and warping through its own third axis. It exists in a different dimension of space than the Right Manifold, so the two can never touch; each asymptotically approaches the shared gap without ever reaching it.

**Interior content — the Ribbon Leg Field**: a pair of tube-shaped ribbons sharing a hip, splayed apart so they cross — "an intersecting set of two ribbons," with one leg's foot resting higher than the other's. A single wall line cuts through the middle of their hip joint as a barrier between them. Carried by the Left Manifold's own anchor and rotation, so it reads as part of the surface rather than a separate floating object.

## Right Manifold
The mirror of the Left Manifold: same nature (a calculus manifold, flat plane crossing multiple dimensions in 3D x/y/z), spinning the opposite direction, existing in its own distinct dimension of space, also asymptotically unable to touch the left.

**Interior content — the Contour Flow**: traced lines that flow across the manifold's surface following the gradient of its own warp function (a literal derivative of the surface), moving perpendicular to that gradient so they trace the manifold's contours rather than collapsing into its peaks and valleys. This is the "complex energy... lines and things that continuously change vectors as they move forward" described for the fields generally.

## Dark Matter (the middle)
"The thin layer spread everywhere that separates the fields of the universe" — "an infinite field transformer with tensor flows to the infinite power." It fills the gap between the Left and Right Manifolds' boundaries, the space neither manifold can ever cross into.

**Visualized as — the Afterbang Drips**: particles falling slowly through the gap, jittering sideways, with rare bright sparks flaring gold — "dripping pieces of cosmic after bang" caught in the layer between the two fields.
