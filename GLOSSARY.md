# Glossary — Dark Matter Manifolds

Terms defined over the course of building `dark-matter-manifolds.html`, sourced from the original prompts in `llm-original-prompt/`. This is the settled definition of the three things on screen.

## Left Manifold
A calculus manifold — a flat plane shape that crosses multiple dimensions, represented in 3D (x, y, z), continuously rotating and warping through its own third axis. It exists in a different dimension of space than the Right Manifold, so the two can never touch; each asymptotically approaches the shared gap without ever reaching it.

**Interior content — the Ribbon Leg Field**: a pair of tube-shaped ribbons sharing a hip, splayed apart so they cross — "an intersecting set of two ribbons," with one leg's foot resting higher than the other's. A single wall line cuts through the middle of their hip joint as a barrier between them. Carried by the Left Manifold's own anchor and rotation, so it reads as part of the surface rather than a separate floating object.

## Right Manifold
The mirror of the Left Manifold: same nature (a calculus manifold, flat plane crossing multiple dimensions in 3D x/y/z), spinning the opposite direction, existing in its own distinct dimension of space, also asymptotically unable to touch the left.

**Interior content — the Contour Flow**: traced lines that flow across the manifold's surface following the gradient of its own warp function (a literal derivative of the surface), moving perpendicular to that gradient so they trace the manifold's contours rather than collapsing into its peaks and valleys. This is the "complex energy... lines and things that continuously change vectors as they move forward" described for the fields generally.

## Dark Matter (the middle)
"The thin layer spread everywhere that separates the fields of the universe" — "an infinite field transformer with tensor flows to the infinite power." It fills the gap between the Left and Right Manifolds' boundaries, the space neither manifold can ever cross into.

**Visualized as — the Afterbang Drips**: particles falling slowly through the gap, jittering sideways, with rare bright sparks flaring gold — "dripping pieces of cosmic after bang" caught in the layer between the two fields.

## The Tunnel
A passage across the gap connecting the Left Manifold to the Right Manifold — a wireframe tube, color-graded from cyan to magenta along its length, pinched to a narrow throat exactly at its center. Its own rotation is slow and independent of both fields, since it belongs to neither dimension.

**The divider**: at the Tunnel's throat sits a glowing violet membrane — Dark Matter made explicit as a physical seal within the passage, "a clear divider," the point neither field's motion can ever push past.

**The Dark Matter Chains**: two chains spanning the full height of the screen — one driven by the Left Manifold's current rotation, one by the Right's. Each field's full rotation state (angX, angY) is collapsed into a single scalar — "a mathematical projection of any shape of things touching turned into a linear form" — and that scalar drives a scroll velocity: how fast, and in which vertical direction, the chain's links travel, with a small baseline speed keeping each one constantly moving even when its field is momentarily still. Because the two chains scroll independently off their own field's sign, they can align (both scrolling up, both down) or oppose. That sync, or lack of it, is the only way these two fields, which can never truly touch, can be said to touch at all.

## Scene Rotation
Grabbing the middle — the gap, where neither field lives — and dragging turns the entire tableau: both Manifolds and the Tunnel together, orbiting freely around their shared center on any axis. Every point in those elements is resolved in a shared world space before this rotation is applied, so it's a real single-camera turn of the whole arrangement rather than each piece spinning independently. The Dark Matter Chains, Afterbang Drips, and coupling-force arrows stay screen-locked — they're already-flattened projections, not 3D content, so they don't turn with the scene.

## Entanglement
Distinct from the coupling force above, which is continuous and causal — one field's velocity steadily leaking into the other's acceleration over time. Entanglement measures how aligned the two fields' velocity directions currently happen to be, regardless of why — and it's rendered as a set of points, each on its own smooth back-and-forth oscillation between the Left Manifold's and Right Manifold's territory, passing through the Tunnel every crossing, each wandering a random noise-driven path rather than tracing a straight line. They're always moving, never gated fully off; alignment between the fields scales how fast and how bright they travel, and the Dark Matter divider flares wider in step. It isn't caused by either field in that moment — it's just correlated. That's what makes it entanglement rather than another coupling force.
